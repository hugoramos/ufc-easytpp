# HoTHP Presentation Design Spec

**Date:** 2026-05-07  
**Author:** Hugo Ramos Soares  
**Occasion:** Graduate seminar (PPGCC/UFC)  
**Audience:** ML-literate professors and students, not necessarily familiar with TPPs  
**Duration:** 45–60 minutes  
**Total slides:** ~38  

---

## Goals

The presentation follows a progressive narrative arc: build the problem step by step until HoTHP emerges as the natural solution. Each block answers "why does this matter?" before explaining "how does it work?". This structure will also serve as the template for the dissertation defense.

---

## Narrative Structure

### Block 1 — Motivation (4 slides)

1. **Title slide** — title, author, UFC, advisor, date
2. **Real-world event sequences** — examples: telemetry, social networks, healthcare, predictive maintenance; images for each domain
3. **What do we want to model?** — predict when the next event occurs and of what type; formal notation $\mathcal{S} = \{(t_i, k_i)\}$
4. **The practical problem** — training on long sequences is expensive because attention costs $O(L^2)$; training on short sequences and deploying on long ones is desirable; the model must not degrade

### Block 2 — Temporal Point Processes and the Hawkes Process (5 slides)

5. **What is a TPP?** — definition of the conditional intensity $\lambda(t \mid \mathcal{H}_t)$; probability of an event in $[t, t+dt)$ given history
6. **The Hawkes Process** — self-excitation; equation $\lambda(t) = \mu + \sum \varphi(t - t')$; physical intuition of aftershocks
7. **Figure** `hawkes_illustration.pdf` — intensity jumping and decaying; multivariate case
8. **How to train?** — negative log-likelihood as loss function; equation
9. **Comparative table** — parametric models (exponential Hawkes, Ogata) vs neural models; what each family can and cannot capture

### Block 3 — Neural Models: from LSTM to Transformer (4 slides)

10. **NHP (Neural Hawkes Process)** — LSTM captures complex dependencies; limitations: slow training, vanishing gradients
11. **Transformers** — parallelism, multi-head attention; why they changed NLP
12. **THP (Transformer Hawkes Process)** — adapts the Transformer for TPPs with sinusoidal temporal encoding
13. **THP limitation** — not time-invariant: same data at different timestamps produces different results

### Block 4 — Length Extrapolation (4 slides)

14. **The problem in LLMs** — perplexity explodes on sequences longer than training; ALiBi and RoPE emerge as answers; reference: Press et al. 2021
15. **Training cost argument** — attention is $O(L^2)$; doubling sequence length quadruples cost; companies need to train short and deploy long
16. **The same problem in TPPs** — NLL degrades outside the training domain; no consolidated solution existed before RoTHP/HoTHP
17. **Formal definition** of *train short, test long* for TPPs

### Block 5 — RoPE and RoTHP (4 slides)

18. **RoPE** — trigonometric rotation of Q and K vectors; relative invariance; rotation cost is $O(L \cdot d)$ (per-event, not per-pair)
19. **RoTHP** — adapts RoPE for TPPs using $\Delta t$ instead of discrete position; achieves time-invariance
20. **RoTHP limitation** — no decay bias; attention does not reflect the self-exciting structure of Hawkes; figure `rope_vs_hope.pdf`
21. **New figure** — side-by-side attention profile of RoTHP vs HoTHP on long sequences; RoTHP attention is flat, HoTHP decays as expected in a Hawkes process

### Block 6 — HoPE and HoTHP (6 slides)

22. **HoPE** — hyperbolic functions with learnable parameter $\theta'$; stable perplexity on long sequences in LLMs; reference: Dai et al. 2025
23. **The hyperbolic kernel** — attention score as $q^\top B(\Delta t) k$ with $\cosh$ and $\sinh$; equation and geometric intuition
24. **Why hyperbolic works for Hawkes** — the decay $e^{-|\delta|\theta'}$ is embedded in the attention mechanism itself; the model learns the Hawkes decay implicitly
25. **HoTHP architecture** — full diagram; figure `architecture.pdf`
26. **Implementation detail** — causal timestamp normalization; numerical stability (avoiding $\cosh$ overflow by distributing the global decay inside the exponentials)
27. **Summary table** — comparison of THP / RoTHP / HoTHP across: time-invariance, decay bias, extrapolation, positional cost, extra parameters

| Property | THP | RoTHP | HoTHP |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time-invariant | No | Yes | Yes |
| Decay bias | No | No | Yes (learned) |
| Extrapolates | No | Partial | Yes |
| Positional cost | $O(L \cdot d)$ | $O(L \cdot d)$ | $O(L^2 \cdot d/2)$ |
| Extra parameters | 0 | 0 | 1 ($\theta'$) |

### Block 7 — Experiments (7 slides)

28. **Experimental setup** — synthetic datasets generated by Hawkes processes (fast-decay and slow-decay); train short, test long protocol; baselines: THP, RoTHP, HoTHP
29. **Figure** `attention_profile.pdf` — attention profiles of each model
30. **Figure** `main_result.pdf` — NLL as a function of test sequence length; HoTHP stable, RoTHP degrades progressively
31. **Figure** `kernel_alignment.pdf` — alignment of the learned kernel with the theoretical Hawkes kernel
32. **Numerical results table** — NLL per model × dataset (fast/slow decay) × sequence length
33. **Computational cost slide** — table: THP and RoTHP have positional cost $O(L \cdot d)$ and relative training time 1x; HoTHP has cost $O(L^2 \cdot d/2)$ and training time 3x. Honest statement: HoTHP takes 3x longer to train than RoTHP under equal conditions, because the hyperbolic kernel must be computed for every event pair.
34. **Break-even analysis** — theoretical argument: if training on sequences $\alpha$ times longer costs $\alpha^2$ times more (due to $O(L^2)$ attention), then HoTHP pays off when $3 < \alpha^2$, i.e., when the target sequence is more than $\approx 1.73 \times$ the training length. The empirical experiment confirming this crossover point has not yet been run and is listed as future work.

### Block 8 — Conclusion (3 slides)

35. **Contributions** — HoTHP: first TPP model with hyperbolic positional encoding; decay implicit in the attention mechanism; stable NLL on sequences longer than training
36. **Limitations and future work** — real datasets; more baselines; empirical break-even experiment; theoretical convergence analysis
37. **Thank you** — contact, repository link, key references

## Pending Experiment

**Break-even experiment:** Train THP, RoTHP, and HoTHP on increasing sequence lengths *without extrapolation* and measure wall-clock training time per epoch. Compare against the extrapolation scenario to find the empirical crossover point where HoTHP becomes more efficient than training a baseline directly on long sequences. This experiment is needed to close the cost-benefit argument and should be added to the presentation once results are available.

## Style and Format

- **Beamer theme:** Madrid (blue/gray, classic academic)
- **Language:** Portuguese throughout (all slide text, titles, descriptions); English only for figure labels, captions, and equation variable names (consistent with the BRACIS paper convention)
- **Writing style:** Explanatory and didactic; no em-dashes or LLM-typical constructs; should read like a student's work
- **Tables:** Use `booktabs` package for clean horizontal rules
- **Equations:** Use `amsmath`; number only the equations referenced in the text
